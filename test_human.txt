Why I Changed My Mind About Taking Notes by Hand

Honestly, I used to think taking notes on my laptop was the obviously better choice. It was faster, my handwriting is terrible, and I could search everything later. But last semester something kind of weird happened — I realized I couldn't actually remember most of what I typed during lectures. I'd have pages of notes and no idea what any of it meant when exam week rolled around.

So I tried an experiment. For my Tuesday morning class (the one I always felt half-asleep in anyway), I forced myself to use a pen. It felt slow and awkward at first. My hand cramped. I missed stuff. But maybe because writing is slower, I had to actually decide what mattered instead of copying sentences verbatim.

I'm not saying handwriting is magic. For math class it's useless — I need to type equations. And sometimes the prof goes so fast that typing is the only way to keep up. But for the boring lecture-heavy courses, the pen somehow worked better for me. I was surprised, because I'd read that study about it years ago and just assumed it wouldn't apply to how my brain works.

Anyway, I'm not throwing my laptop away. I just wish someone had told me earlier that the "better" tool depends on what you're trying to learn, not on which one feels more efficient in the moment.
